    1. Reading the NDSI panel — NDSI here is MNDWI, so water is positive and sand is negative. Colour = (NDSI+1)/2 on a blue-white-red scale: medium blue (NDSI -0.30) = strong bare sand; light blue (-0.18) = typical sand in our scene; very light blue (-0.10) = sand threshold; white (0.00) = boundary; pinkish white (+0.05) = water. Our values only span about -0.30 to +0.05, so water is the whitest patches, not vivid red. Always confirm with truecolour.

    2. The critical distinction — NDSI tells you WHERE sand is, not whether it is being mined. Even NOT MINING chips are 16-55% sand. Paint only sand that also shows extraction damage in truecolour.

    3. Signatures to look for — pits (dark angular holes), spoil heaps (lighter mounds beside pits), machinery tracks (straight parallel lines), freshly cut edges (straight, geometric sand-water boundary), barges (small dark rectangles on water), texture contrast (smooth vs rough patches). Natural bars have soft curved edges and uniform texture; mining makes hard angular straight edges.

    4. Workflow — (a) find the blue sand in NDSI; (b) inspect those areas in truecolour; (c) paint the damaged parts, not all sand; (d) check the hint — MINING chips should have substantial disturbed area, NOT MINING chips nearly empty, but if a MINING chip looks natural paint little; (e) save and move on.

    5. Common mistakes — painting all blue pixels (that is just sand); painting white/red water (not mining); leaving MINING chips empty (small pits matter); painting the whole chip because the hint says MINING (our chips are 60-84% sand, not 100%); tracing the NDSI outline exactly (mining patches are irregular).

    Rule of thumb: if you can see a geometry nature would not make — straight cuts, angular holes, parallel tracks, piles beside holes — on bare sand (blue NDSI), paint it. Otherwise leave it blank.
